# Day 1 &middot; _Rest for the Weary_

We’ve learned about special feast days of the year and all the celebrations. Today, we’re going to learn about special years! 

The Torah contains some beautiful social principles that demand respect for human dignity, equality, and justice…but also defies good economic sense. But God’s math is not ours. Let’s dive in! 

Jubilee—where debts are forgiven, slaves are released, and property is returned to its original family owners. God’s concern for the poor and disadvantaged in society is at the center of this legislation. 

God starts right off with the sabbath, but this time, it’s the land that is going to observe it.

```
Leviticus 25:2 “Speak to the Israelites and say to them: ‘When you enter the land I am going to give you, the ___ itself must observe a sabbath to the LORD.’”
```

This will be a completely different kind of Sabbath because it will introduce a seven-year cycle that concludes with a Sabbath year. Before you make your reservations to spend a year in the Bahamas, this is not a rest for the people—it is a Sabbath for the land of Israel. 

God is giving instructions for letting the land of Israel rest by stopping agriculture for one out of every seven years. Farmers were to let the ground go fallow. 

```
List all the things they were not to do in Leviticus 25:4-5: “Do not ___ your fields or ___ your vineyards. Do not ___ what grows of itself or ___ the grapes of your untended vines. The land is to have a year of ___.”
```

This is not a mandate for the United States or Europe to let their gardens and fields go unplanted during the Sabbatical Year. These laws apply only to the land of Israel. 
Does land get tired? Why does it need to rest? Well, the word Shabbat doesn’t mean “to rest,” like taking a nap; it means “to cease” or “to desist.” God wanted the land to have a year to stop production and creation. 

Now, that would take a big leap of faith for the Israelite farmer! What would they do? Well, God anticipated that question. Read Leviticus 25:20-21. What was God’s response? ______

This reminds me of God's similar provision with the people when He instituted the Sabbath to them in Exodus. 

```
Exodus 16:22: “On the sixth day, they gathered _________ as much”—God provided for their Sabbath on the seventh day.
```

Could you trust God for a blessing big enough to yield enough for three years? I’m sure many people could not believe…even though God was still feeding them manna! Here’s the thing: It’s easy to think the world will stop spinning if we stop working. 

God is reminding the Israelites that rest and renewal go hand-in-hand. Do you need some rest and renewal today? 

```
Jesus says in Matthew 11:28, “Come to Me, all you who are ___ and ___, and I will give you ___.”
```

As I’m writing this, I’m laughing to myself because just a few hours ago, I found out that I will need a second breast cancer surgery. I want to return to work and be “normal”...whatever that is! What will happen if I’m not there? The simple fact is that life will go on. 

The Bible teaches us to trust God rather than our own efforts. It is so easy to get caught up in the trap of materialism. We measure the quality of our lives based on our possessions. How silly is that? Do you really own your designer clothing or prestigious education? Can they be taken away, stolen, or deleted? If so, it’s not yours to begin with. 

In reality, the people ignored this gap year: the land’s Sabbath rest. There is no record in the Bible of the children of Israel keeping the Sabbath year. In fact, they neglected it for 490 years! Can you figure out how many Sabbath year rests they missed? Let’s do a little calculation: 490 divided by 7 is seventy. The land missed its Sabbath rest for 70 years.  If you ever wondered why the Babylonian captivity lasted 70 years, that’s why! God was saying, “You owe me 70 years that the land must rest. You didn’t do that, so I’m kicking you out of the land so it can rest without you.”   

```
2 Chronicles 36:21 says, “The ____  enjoyed its sabbath rests; all the time of its desolation it rested until the seventy years were completed in fulfillment of the word of the Lord spoken by Jeremiah.”
```

The people didn’t take God seriously for 490 years. So now God is going to weigh in on it. 

When Jesus went to the synagogue in Luke 4, He quoted the book of Isaiah 61:1-2, saying, “The Spirit of the Lord is on me, because he has anointed me to proclaim good news to the poor. He has sent me to proclaim freedom for the prisoners and recovery of sight for the blind, to set the oppressed free, to proclaim the year of the Lord’s favor.”

When the people heard this, they knew He was announcing, “Your Jubilee has arrived. I am here to set the captives free.” 

How did the people respond when Jesus read this in Luke 4:20-21? _______

Knowing what you know now about Leviticus, what do you think was going on in the people’s heads when Jesus read those words and proclaimed, “Today this scripture is fulfilled in your hearing.”?

Listen, if we don’t obey God’s rules, He will make an opportunity to obey. The land WILL rest, and it will not only rest but enjoy its rest! God commanded it. God said, “Let there be light,” and there was light. He commanded it into existence. 

Philippians 2:10-11 says that in the name of Jesus, EVERY knee WILL bow, and EVERY tongue WILL confess that Jesus is Lord.

If God commands it, it will happen.

# Day 2 &middot; _Jubilee, the Holy Year of Celebration!_

Guest author: David Demian

<span style="font-size: 1.25em"><i class="fa-solid fa-book-open"></i>&nbsp; **Read** |</span> Leviticus 25:8-17.

&nbsp;

After seven Sabbath years (49 years), in the fiftieth year, the Year of Jubilee starts on Yom Kippur (The Day of Atonement), so they began this year repenting of their sins (remember that). The Jubilee year was a year of celebration! It even started with the sound of the shofar blaring throughout the land. In fact, the Hebrew word for jubilee, תְּרוּעָה (Teruah), means a blast of joy.  That year, all the slaves went free, all the debts were canceled, and all the land was reverted to its original owners. Could you imagine your debts being forgiven, and if you sold your land away, you got it back? It is fascinating to me how God made sure that rich people could not take full advantage of poor people in debt. 

There are many interpretations of the Jubilee Year. The Israelites were an agricultural-based society, and to go two years (including the sabbatical year) without growing crops would be crazy! Some people suggest the jubilee year lasted only fifty days, so it makes more sense to them.

This reminded me of a conversation with a friend a few years ago. He said, "I don’t think all parts of the Bible are true because it's impossible and makes no sense.” But God is not bound to our universe. Think of it this way. If I make a box, am I bound to it? No. I can step into and out of the box, but the box and I are separate. It is the same with God and our universe. He and our universe are separate. 

Have you ever tried to mentally limit God's power because it is impossible in our universe?

So what does the Bible say? 

```
Leviticus 25:21: “I will command my blessing on you in the sixth year so that it will produce a crop ___ for ___ years.”
```

God is not natural; He is UN-natural. He makes the impossible possible.

Now, you might be thinking, “OK, David. It is nice thinking of the Jubilee year happening today, but what does this ancient ‘holy year’ have to do with me? This is 2024 AD, not 1300 BC.”

Jesus is our Jubilee! 

Use your (hopefully by this point) handy blueletterbible.org skill and look up the word “liberty” in verse 10. It is the Hebrew word דְּרוֹר (Deror). This word usually refers to the Jubilee year. The same Hebrew word דְּרוֹר (Deror) is also used in Isaiah 61:1-2 (see below). This verse is also referring to the year of Jubilee. But this verse is a prophecy. Now read Luke 4:18-21.

When Jesus was on earth, the year of Jubilee was during His years of ministry. It could have been in the Year of Jubilee when Jesus entered the Nazareth synagogue and read from Isaiah 61:1-2. 

```
“The Spirit of the Sovereign LORD is on me because the LORD has ___ me to proclaim the ___ news to the poor. He has sent me to ___ up the brokenhearted, to proclaim ___ to the captives and release from darkness for the ___, to proclaim the ___ of the LORD’s favor.” (Isaiah 61:1-2)
```

He was announcing, “Your Jubilee has arrived! I am here to set the captives free!” 

We all have a massive spiritual debt, but like the Hebrew slaves, we can’t repay it. We have sold ourselves to sin as the Hebrew slaves sold themselves. However, we have a Jubilee Year. Just like the Year of Jubilee freed slaves from their physical bondage, Jesus freed us from our spiritual bondage.  He is our Jubilee year!

Side note: Earlier, I mentioned how the Jubilee starts on Yom Kippur (The Day of Atonement). Do you see the connection? After we repent, we have our Jubilee (forgiveness).

# Day 3 &middot; _Land and People_

Something to remember in all these rules and regulations is that the children of Israel are STILL in the wilderness…they have been in the wilderness for under a year. It's hard to believe because it seems like it’s been so long. They don’t know this, but they still have many more years to go! Aren’t you so glad God doesn’t give you all the details up front?

God is preparing them for when they DO get the land…and He’s not even preparing the adults; He’s preparing the children. No adults listening to God right now will enter the Promised Land because of disobedience and lack of faith. 

```
Leviticus 25:18: “Follow my decrees and be careful to obey my laws, and you will live ___ in the land.”
```

Leviticus 25:18-34 deals with land issues. 

History has been amazingly accurate for verse 18. When the Israelites walked in the ways of the LORD, the land was miraculously productive. During Bible times, it was the breadbasket of the Middle East. Its produce was renowned for its quality and quantity. Records from Babylon, Persia, and Rome all discuss the fruits, vegetables, and grains produced in the Holy Lands. 

Interestingly, every time the Israelites were exiled, the land stopped producing. Mark Twain even wrote about the barrenness of Palestine. When Israel was not in the land, the land responded by going fallow. 

God would take the land from the wicked Canaanites and turn it over to Israel as the Promised Land. Once God’s people entered the land, the land would be productive only for God’s people. 

Here’s a more recent example of this. In 1967, Israel regained control of the desolate Gaza Strip. Between 1967 and 2005, the Jewish farmland supplied ⅓ of all the produce raised in Israel. In 2005, when the Palestinians got the land, it became barren again. This is a God-principle that science, the UN, or the United States cannot change. 

In Leviticus 25:35-55, the narrative changes from property to human property: people who had become bondservants or slaves. 

Bond servants were people who had fallen on hard times and owed a debt—so they worked for their wages like a bound employee…working for the creditor. However, the longest period they could be indebted to their master was until the year of Jubilee. At that point, whatever debt they owed was canceled. 

```
The principle was found in Leviticus 25:42. “Because the Israelites are ___ servants, whom I brought out of Egypt, they must not be sold as slaves.” The people belonged to God. It was God who redeemed them from Egypt. God purchased their freedom from slavery.
```

To be clear—and this is important—the only people who could be redeemed and freed of their debts were those under the covenant God made with Israel. Foreigners who wanted to be a part of Israel were allowed to do so, but they had to be under the covenant. 

I love this book because it shows how God was making a way far in advance of our needing it. Think of it: God planted the tree’s seed before Zaccheus needed to see Jesus. 
Verse 25 is one of those seeds that will be planted now but used later in history. 

```
Leviticus 25:25: “If one of your fellow Israelites becomes poor and sells some of their property, their nearest relative is to come and ___ what they have sold.”
```

Jubilee was somewhat of a problem. A man could become poor, lose his portion of land, and not get it back for 50 years. Depending on how old he was, he may never see that land again in his lifetime. So, to fix that problem, God made a provision to recover the land through a relative called a “goel,” or a kinsman redeemer. 

This kinsman redeemer would need to be a relative, have the money (no discounts), and be willing to buy it back. Then, they would step in and bring that land back to the family. 

Does this bring back any memories of other Bible stories you’ve heard? It is the story of Ruth. If you like a good romance novel, you must read all that Naomi does to teach Ruth how to catch a husband. It’s charming and a really beautiful story! (And only four chapters, so BONUS!)

A woman named Naomi got married to a man named Elimelech. They got married and got a plot of land in Bethlehem to start their lives. But their dreams were shattered when famine hit Bethlehem. They left to go to Moab, and it was there that their two sons got married to Moabite women: Ruth and Orpah. While they were trying to recover, Elimelech and his two sons, Mahlon and Chilion, died. (As a side note: their son’s names mean “sickly” and “wasting.” Who would name their kids that???)

When Naomi heard that the famine was over in Bethlehem, she decided to go home. The girls wanted to go with her, but she told them to stay in Moab. Ruth insists on going with her mother-in-law by saying these famous words: “Where you go, I will go. Where you live, I will live. Your people will be my people, and your God will be my God.” (This is one of the most famous verses recited at weddings.)  

But Naomi tries to push her away by saying, “I’m a bitter old lady. Don’t come.” But Ruth goes anyway. 

Back in Bethlehem, Naomi discovered that she had a relative named Boaz who owned some land. She does a little matchmaking, and eventually, Boaz becomes the “Goel,” the kinsman redeemer who buys the land back for the family and marries Ruth. They get married and live happily ever after. 

The book ends with a genealogy. Ruth 4:17: “And they named him [Ruth and Boaz’s son] Obed. He was the father of Jesse, the father of David.” And through David (many years later), the TRUE kinsman redeemer was born—Jesus! 

The goel needed to be a relative, have the money, be able, and be willing. They couldn’t be forced. That’s the condition. They paid the money, took the scroll, broke the seal, and claimed the land. 

Let’s fast-forward to Revelation 5. John has a vision. God is sitting on a throne. In His hand is a scroll sealed with seven seals. In Revelation 5:2, an angel says, “Who is worthy to break the seals and open the scroll?” But no one in heaven, earth, or under the earth could open the scroll or even look inside it. And John wept and wept. 

John was looking at the title deed to the earth. God made the earth and gave it to man to have dominion over it. But man forfeited the earth to Satan. That’s why Paul calls the devil the “ruler of this world.” It’s also why Satan told Jesus, “Kneel down and worship me. I’ll give you the earth, for it is mine.” Jesus did not argue with him on that point. 

So, the earth is under the control of the god of this age. No one can open and reclaim the title deed. So John wept…and then he saw The Lamb that was slain!!! Jesus! Jesus is worthy to open the scroll and claim the earth! 

He is able. He is willing. He’s related. God had to become a man to be related. And how did He purchase the scroll? With His blood! He wasn’t forced. He willingly did it to buy us back. Leviticus ties a bow at the end of the entire Bible!
