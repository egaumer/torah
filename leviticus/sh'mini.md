# Day 1 &middot; _Holy Fire_

<span style="font-size: 1.25em"><i class="fa-solid fa-book-open"></i>&nbsp; **Read** |</span> Leviticus 9:1-21.

&nbsp;

This is Aaron and his sons’ first day on the job! They began their ministry in chapter 9. At this point, Moses will fade into the background, and the priesthood will take center stage. This is a big moment because it is the very first sacrificial ritual performed by the newly ordained priesthood of Israel. 

This chapter has burnt and sin offerings—first on behalf of the priests and then for the people. Again, this demonstrates a principle that we can use today. Our only approach to God is through sacrifice. There are two things we need: blood and a mediator. Hallelujah! Jesus Christ is both of those things. Jesus is both the priest and the sacrifice. 

What day did they begin their work (verse 1)? ___

A week had passed. They have been in the tabernacle all that time. The number 8 signifies a new beginning. Scripture has many patterns of 7; each has a new beginning on the 8th day. It’s a spiritual do-over. 

Do you need a spiritual do-over? When you are ready, God is ready. Let’s see what happens on this 8th day. Get ready because God is about to show up to church today! 

There are five sacrifices: the burnt offering, grain offering, peace/fellowship offering, sin offering, and trespass offering. The first four are offered on the first day of the priesthood. 

Remember, the priest's function is to represent the people before God. He is NOT a representative of God here. He’s bearing their names on his shoulder and over his heart. He’s offering a sacrifice on behalf of the people to God. (Later in Scripture, we will see the opposite happen. A person who represents God to the people is called a prophet.)

In chapter 8, it says five times that Moses “consecrated” them. Another word you may be familiar with is “sanctify.” It means to be set apart, holy, treated as sacred, and dedicated. Moses set this group of men apart to be holy and dedicated to the LORD. The men obeyed and started working immediately.

How exciting! Aaron brought the offerings for the people: the sin offering (vs. 15), the burnt offering (vs. 16), the grain offering (vs. 17), and the fellowship offering (vs. 18).

Holy fire came out from the presence of the LORD and consumed all of the burnt offerings and the fat portions on the altar. This fire, this fire that came from heaven and lit up the outer court and the altar of sacrifice, is the fire that they were commanded to keep burning! God started this fire, not man, but man will keep it burning. 

This means that hot coals must be preserved wherever they go in the wilderness. This fire was holy. It was set apart. This divine fire, and only this divine fire, can be used to burn up sacrifices. The past seven days were a “rehearsal” of sorts. The fire was man-made and was acceptable by God for the ordination service. Once the ordination was complete, however, God re-ignited the fire on the altar with “holy fire,” from that moment on, no man-made fire could be used. 

In the same way, Jesus sent us the Holy Spirit. We have a duty and a responsibility to keep that fire burning.

I learned of an interesting connection in the word “calf” found in verses 2, 3, and 8. This Hebrew word, “egel,” was first mentioned in Exodus 32 when Aaron fashioned the golden calf. Note: this incident happened only weeks earlier. But perhaps God is making a point and a connection for Aaron and his sons. There is a contrast between God’s system of pure worship and the Egyptians’ pagan system of worship. In God’s system, the animal was never worshiped as in pagan worship. The animal was a sacrifice for the benefit of man. All the other bull sacrifices in Leviticus are of a bull over three years old. This bull was a particular-year-old bull (egel).

Imagine yourself in that place. You are in a sea of people out in the beautiful desert. The location of where you are to Mount Sinai acts like a megaphone that amplifies Aaron’s voice. You see the smoke rising from the Altar of Sacrifice as Aaron steps up, raises his hands, and declares a blessing on you on behalf of the God of the universe. 

The crowd gasps as they try to take it all in. Their only reaction was to fall on their faces—out of fear, respect, gratitude, honor…all the things! 

With that blessing, Moses and Aaron entered the Tabernacle, and God's presence appeared to all the people! Wowzer! God was pleased that all of His instructions and commands were followed, and He showed up in all His glory! 

We don’t have the words God said in Leviticus, but we do in Numbers 6:22:
