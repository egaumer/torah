<img width="430" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/813870b1-13a2-422b-b5ec-126a91f50225"> https://youtu.be/h6bPAyPCYu4 

We are back to discovering what is clean and unclean, but it becomes much more personal this time. Let's look not at what goes into the body but what is outside of the body. Who knew there could be so many skin ailments? The priests were not just serving as dermatologists but as public health officials. Keeping the people "clean" was always for the good of the nation of Israel as a whole. These carefully defined criteria were designed for diagnosing scientifically, not by intuition or guessing.

The word leprosy in this passage includes a variety of dermatological conditions. It could be an allergy, psoriasis, eczema, or full-blown leprosy. This chapter covers it all. 

God gave the nation of Israel the gift of quarantining the contagious. No other nation at that time understood or practiced isolation for the greater good of its people. In David Guzik's commentary, he states: “These judgments were made with a concern for the benefit of the afflicted person, but with an even greater concern for the health of the community from the outbreak of disease. All judgments were based on sound medical diagnosis and concern for the afflicted person, but with an even greater concern for the health of the community from an outbreak of the condition. God is involved in all aspects of human life.” 

It is interesting that leprosy, as we understand it today, may not be what the priests were dealing with. Many scholars believe these skin conditions were only present in biblical times, but it is unclear. Nonetheless, illnesses such as smallpox, measles, and scarlet fever might start with a skin condition considered leprosy—and the person would be isolated for the necessary time until the condition cleared up. This quarantine helped prevent the spread of these kinds of diseases among the people of Israel. 

Every skin issue was looked at as leprosy because of its deadly potential. According to David Guzik:

“When leprosy first appears on a victim’s skin, it begins as small, red spots. Before too long they get bigger, start to turn white, having a shiny or scaly appearance. Soon, the spots spread over the entire body, and the hair begins to fall out—first from the head and then even from the eyebrows. As things worsen, the fingernails and toenails become loose, rotting, and eventually falling off. Then the joints of fingers and toes begin to rot and start to fall off piece by piece. In the mouth, the gums start shrinking and are unable to hold teeth, so several teeth are often lost. Leprosy keeps eating away at the face until the nose is literally gone, and the palate and even eyes rot—and the victim wastes away until death.” 

Leprosy is like sin in many ways. There are some good reasons why many ancient rabbis considered a leper as someone already dead. Leprosy is like sin in that: 


* It begins as nothing. It’s insignificant, just a little spot—no big deal.
* It is painless in its first stages.
* It multiplies. It’s highly contagious—which is why you need to be separated.
* It often remits for a while and then returns.
* It numbs the senses—one cannot feel in the afflicted area. You may have been sensitive at one point, but now you don’t even notice. Sin is like that. You can become oblivious—not even phased.
* It causes decay and deformity. It affects us deeply. It’s a bacteria that ravages your body.
* It eventually gives a person a repulsive appearance. The leper becomes physically and emotionally shunned.
* Leprosy is always deadly. It is incurable and will kill you by the inch. Apart from a touch from God, there is no hope.

  But God… Jesus not only touched the lepers in His time, but He healed them.

Mold was not just a problem for Israel in the wilderness; anyone with a shower in their bathroom knows mold can creep in without our permission. According to the CDC - Center for Disease Control and Prevention,

"Mold is found both indoors and outdoors. Mold can enter your home through open doorways, windows, vents, and heating and air conditioning systems. Mold in the air outside can also attach itself to clothing, shoes, and pets. When mold spores drop on places with excessive moisture, such as where leakage may have occurred in roofs, pipes, walls, plant pots, or where there has been flooding, they will grow. Many building materials provide suitable nutrients that encourage mold to grow. Wet cellulose materials, including paper and paper products, cardboard, ceiling tiles, wood, and wood products, are particularly conducive to the growth of some molds. Other materials commonly support mold growth, such as dust, paints, wallpaper, insulation, drywall, carpet, fabric, and upholstery."

The CDC also states that there was sufficient evidence to link indoor exposure to mold with upper respiratory tract symptoms, cough, and wheeze in otherwise healthy people, with asthma symptoms in people with asthma, and with hypersensitivity pneumonitis in individuals susceptible to that immune-mediated condition.
