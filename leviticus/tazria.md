# _Tazria_ &sdot; צַו &sdot; Leviticus 12-13

Learning with guest author Karen Seddon. 

This reading from the Torah is called Tazria, which means she bears seed. We are introduced to God's instructions to Moses for the people of Israel about the purification rituals for mothers following childbirth.

Even though Chapter 12 deals with childbirth and hints at what women must do during their menstrual cycle, we will learn many more details in Leviticus 15. For now, let's look at what it was like to be a woman about to bear a child.

Get ready for a detailed yet amazing gift from God, Who taught the people of Israel how to deal with boils and burns, skin diseases of all types, and even what to do when mold becomes a menace.

Why eight days for a boy? Well, we know seven days would be the number of perfection, and the 8th day is a day of new beginnings! Plus, God knew that on the 8th day after a male child is born, his body’s ability to clot blood reaches its perfect rate. It is the safest day to circumcise a male child. Now, we use vitamin K injections to help that, but there was no such thing in those days. They had to go off of God’s perfect timing.

If all of this isolation and uncleanness seems harsh, it is because we look at life through our 21st-century lives. God is always wiser than us and is incredibly gracious. Being "unclean" during our periods or after childbirth would be a great gift. There is no shame in having a menstrual cycle or giving birth, but being "unclean" gives the woman, the new mother, a time to heal, a time to bond with the baby, a time to be free from the daily responsibilities that women have. It also points us back to the Garden of Eden when Adam and Eve sinned.

At the end of the purification time, a mother brings an offering to the priest at the Tent of Meeting. 

 - a year-old lamb (burnt offering)
 - young pigeon or dove (sin offering)
 - the priest then makes atonement for her
 - the mother is ceremonially clean from her flow of blood
 - if she cannot afford the lamb, she can bring two doves or two pigeons (one for the burnt offering - and one for the sin offering)
 - mother will be clean

Joseph and Mary were too poor to afford a lamb because they were impoverished, so they brought two birds. He came to change lives and bring healing and salvation for rich, poor, men, women, every nation, and every tongue. Wow!
