# _Metzora_ &sdot; מְּצֹרָע &sdot; Leviticus 14-15

The 28th reading from the Torah is Metzora. It is taken from the second verse in the reading.

```
“These are the regulations for any ___ person at the time of their ceremonial cleansing when they are brought to the priest.” (Leviticus 14:2)
```

Metzora means leper. A person diagnosed with leprosy is called a metzora, and he enters a state of ritual impurity that forces him out of society. The Torah does not offer a cure for leprosy, but it does offer a procedure to purify a person who has recovered from leprosy so that they may return to a state of ritual purity. The priest is to examine the afflicted person. If the priest is satisfied that the leprosy is gone, he begins the purification process.

# Day 1 &middot; The Fountain of Youth

As tedious as Leviticus 13 was concerning the details of tara’at, chapter 14 unveils the root and purpose of these ritual purification procedures. Chapter 14 will show us how they become clean and acceptable to Yahweh once again! 

The rituals are similar to those in chapter 8 (consecration of the priests). This is not a coincidence. Look closely because they are a shadow, a precise pattern, that Jesus would fulfill 13 centuries later. 

<span style="font-size: 1.25em"><i class="fa-solid fa-book-open"></i>&nbsp; **Read** |</span> Leviticus 14:1-7.

Leviticus teaches us that God is holy and set apart. The Israelites need to be in a state of holiness, themselves, to enter His presence (this is called “clean” or “pure”). God’s presence was off-limits to anyone who was not in a holy state (“unclean” or “impure”). There were many ways an Israelite could become unclean: contact with bodily fluids, having a skin disease, touching mold or fungus, touching a dead body…among others. The idea was that you become impure when you touch death—as opposed to God’s holiness, which is life. Being impure was not the same as being evil. It was a part of life. Women had babies; people passed away—all normal things. Impurity was temporary in most cases. But you absolutely could not enter God’s presence with symbols of death and impurity on you. 

In the first 32 verses of this chapter, you are told what the priests do if someone cleansed of leprosy comes to them. If a person was healed, list several things that the priests would have to do: ____

The priest takes two birds. One is killed, symbolizing what would happen to the leper if God had not worked a miracle…he would have been dead. The living bird symbolizes a do-over, a new chance at life, restoration, and freedom. The leper was separated for a long time, but now the bird can spread its wings and be reintroduced into the community. The evangelical concept of “born again” did NOT originate in the New Testament. The idea of “born again” is an Old Testament pattern reintroduced with Jesus. Jesus fulfilled the meaning. 

There is also symbolism of the death and resurrection of Christ in these birds. The first bird died. He was killed, blood was shed—that’s the death of Christ. Atonement was made. After being dipped in blood, the second bird is released in the open fields to fly—Jesus, by His blood, ascended into heaven. 

As far as the hyssop, cedar wood, and scarlet thread, they worked together. Perhaps the cedar was a handle, and the hyssop branches were tied onto it like a paintbrush. The scarlet dye was from a particular worm called a Cermes Vermilion. When the female lays her eyes, the larvae feed off her body.

<span style="font-size: 1.25em"><i class="fa-solid fa-book-open"></i>&nbsp; **Read** |</span> Psalm 23.

In this passage, we are introduced to the Lord as our shepherd. The shepherd is not a distant figure but an intimate, caring guide who tends to the needs of his flock. A good shepherd ensures the well-being of the sheep under his care. 

```
“You ___ my head with ___.” (Psalm 23:5)
```
This seemingly small detail carries profound significance. In ancient Middle Eastern context, shepherds would anoint the heads of their sheep for several reasons: 

  1—**Healing**: Sheep are susceptible to various ailments, including insect bites. The most common is the sheep bot fly. The adult bot lays eggs near the opening of the sheep’s nostrils or on the wool near the face. Sheep can also pick up the eggs on their noses and ears while grazing. The warmth of the sheep’s body triggers the eggs to hatch. 

Once in the nasal passages, the larvae may crawl up the sheep’s sinuses and move to the base of the brain. This causes not only discomfort and irritation but also neurological symptoms in sheep. Because of the pain, they will often hit their heads against objects. The remedy? Oil. The anointing of their heads makes it difficult for the insects to latch onto the wool. 

We also need healing from the little things that can get into our brains. This week, I will have breast cancer surgery. The biggest obstacle for me is the invisible head game that goes on. I need an anointing. And so do you. 

  2—**Wound prevention**: Scratching and biting insects can open wounds on sheep’s skin. Anointing with oil creates a protective barrier. Do you need God’s protection today? 
  
  3—**Soothing skin irritations**: Oil is soothing on sheep’s skin. It alleviates existing irritations and can be very comforting to the animal. 
  
  4—**Wool quality**: Healthy sheep produce better-quality wool. Anointing with oil indirectly contributes to maintaining the quality of their wool by protecting them from all the above issues. 
  
Do you need an anointing?
