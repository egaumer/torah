# _Metzora_ &sdot; מְּצֹרָע &sdot; Leviticus 14-15

The 28th reading from the Torah is Metzora. It is taken from the second verse in the reading.

```
“These are the regulations for any ___ person at the time of their ceremonial cleansing when they are brought to the priest.” (Leviticus 14:2)
```

Metzora means leper. A person diagnosed with leprosy is called a metzora, and he enters a state of ritual impurity that forces him out of society. The Torah does not offer a cure for leprosy, but it does offer a procedure to purify a person who has recovered from leprosy so that they may return to a state of ritual purity. The priest is to examine the afflicted person. If the priest is satisfied that the leprosy is gone, he begins the purification process.

# Day 1 &middot; The Fountain of Youth

As tedious as Leviticus 13 was concerning the details of tara’at, chapter 14 unveils the root and purpose of these ritual purification procedures. Chapter 14 will show us how they become clean and acceptable to Yahweh once again! 

The rituals are similar to those in chapter 8 (consecration of the priests). This is not a coincidence. Look closely because they are a shadow, a precise pattern, that Jesus would fulfill 13 centuries later.

Leviticus teaches us that God is holy and set apart. The Israelites need to be in a state of holiness, themselves, to enter His presence (this is called “clean” or “pure”). God’s presence was off-limits to anyone who was not in a holy state (“unclean” or “impure”). There were many ways an Israelite could become unclean: contact with bodily fluids, having a skin disease, touching mold or fungus, touching a dead body…among others. The idea was that you become impure when you touch death—as opposed to God’s holiness, which is life. Being impure was not the same as being evil. It was a part of life. Women had babies; people passed away—all normal things. Impurity was temporary in most cases. But you absolutely could not enter God’s presence with symbols of death and impurity on you. 

In the first 32 verses of this chapter, you are told what the priests do if someone cleansed of leprosy comes to them. If a person was healed, list several things that the priests would have to do: ____

The priest takes two birds. One is killed, symbolizing what would happen to the leper if God had not worked a miracle…he would have been dead. The living bird symbolizes a do-over, a new chance at life, restoration, and freedom. The leper was separated for a long time, but now the bird can spread its wings and be reintroduced into the community. The evangelical concept of “born again” did NOT originate in the New Testament. The idea of “born again” is an Old Testament pattern reintroduced with Jesus. Jesus fulfilled the meaning. 

There is also symbolism of the death and resurrection of Christ in these birds. The first bird died. He was killed, blood was shed—that’s the death of Christ. Atonement was made. After being dipped in blood, the second bird is released in the open fields to fly—Jesus, by His blood, ascended into heaven. 

As far as the hyssop, cedar wood, and scarlet thread, they worked together. Perhaps the cedar was a handle, and the hyssop branches were tied onto it like a paintbrush. The scarlet dye was from a particular worm called a Cermes Vermilion. When the female lays her eyes, the larvae feed off her body.

The bird that is killed is done so over ________ water (verse 5). Some versions say “fresh water,” others say “running water.” The word in Hebrew is hay, meaning “living water.” The term “living water” was entirely understood by the Israelites. Rivers dried up. Jesus called Himself “living water.” He is the REAL source of purification, and the source is unlimited. Here is another New Testament idea that began in the Torah.

What?! He is separated from the community for a reason. The priest goes into an unclean environment to inspect him, not to heal him. He is only a witness to God’s healing. 

The word for leprosy is tsara’at. It can mean two things: 1) a general skin disease (which can get worse or better over time), or 2) a fatal form of leprosy (Hansen’s disease)—that affects your nerves and respiratory tract and eventually causes you to lose limbs. If it’s the first definition of leprosy (see above), then we can understand that the priest could go and declare them clean. But if it were Hansen’s disease, there was no known cure. It makes for an interesting ritual law if no healing is possible. Interestingly enough, there were only two recorded healings of leprosy in the Old Testament: Miriam and Naaman.

The lesson is simple: we often seek profound solutions to our problems. But God invites us to a simpler path of obedience and faith. Naaman’s healing came through the simple act of dipping himself seven times in the Jordan. The waters of the Jordan held no magical powers; instead, Naaman’s willingness to follow God’s simple instruction brought about his restoration. The true fountain of youth is our willingness to obey God’s seemingly simple commands, even when they challenge our expectations. Our simple obedience to God can bring extraordinary transformations in our lives.

I am writing this study on Christmas night. It seems strange reading Leviticus on a night when we remember a babe in the manger, wise men and shepherds watching over their flocks. But Leviticus fits perfectly. When Jesus rose from the dead, He met two men on a road to Emmaus (Luke 24) and went back through the writings of Moses (which would have included this book), and He explained to them what was said in all the Scriptures concerning Himself. These things Jesus would have taught would have been the anticipated birth of the Messiah.

We’re all sinners. But before we came to Christ, we may not have realized it. When you looked around you, you saw that others were also sinners. You may have thought, “Well, I’m not as bad as that guy over there, so I’m good, right?” Until you met Jesus and saw a perfectly clean and holy man. Next to Him, we look filthy. We cannot compare any human to Jesus. In His light, our sin is evident and disgusting. 

Our vacuum has a light on the front. Does yours? It’s all good until I come to a dark corner. I see every Penny and Mojo hair when that light shines on it! What looked like a clean floor before was suddenly exposed to light. In the same way, the vacuum light shows us the dirt, and God’s light shows us our sins.

Leprosy in the Bible is portrayed as ___. It was a genuine skin disease and a symbol of sin. There was no known cure for it. If you had leprosy, you were going to die—unless there was a miraculous intervention. It was incurable; it was your death sentence. And beyond that, it brought you separation from the rest of the community. You would be quarantined outside of the camp and would have to be constantly crying out, “Unclean! Stay away!” There would be no intimate fellowship with friends or family whatsoever. Their uncleanliness would infect others. The entire community would never get near you. No sacrifice would heal you. The priests were not doctors; they only dealt with your status in the nation, not your medical symptoms. The only thing you could look forward to would be your death. 

This kind of paints a horrible picture, right? But it is the same with sin. I’ve heard people say that when they go to hell, it’s going to be a big party with all their friends, dancing with the devil. Really?! I hate to break it to you, but there will be no partying. 

The wages of sin is death. There will also be no rehabilitation program to change your status. What you will get is utter separation from anyone you love and eternal separation from God. You can’t clean up your sin. It’s hopeless. The only way to change that picture is for you to accept that you are a sinner and receive God’s free gift of salvation now. (This is not something to wait on. We are not all promised a long life with time to repent.) 

It’s the same with us. Our uncleanliness keeps us separated from God. God’s holiness cannot be near sin. But Jesus did not keep Himself separated from our sin and uncleanliness. Here’s the cool thing. Jesus left the actual presence of a Holy God and came straight to our unclean mess. He did this to show us that no one is outside His reach. Jesus instantly healed people with leprosy, illnesses, and other ailments. He didn’t need a priest to wait eight days to check them. Jesus could change people’s symptoms AND their status! Jesus could declare a person healed AND ritually clean! That’s really, really good news! 

Our unclean state cannot be spread to Jesus. When we come to Him, we do not make Jesus unclean. The opposite is true. Jesus comes to us amid our disease and unrighteousness and spreads His holiness to us. 

After Miriam (Moses’ sister) is healed from leprosy, no recorded incident of any Israelite in the Old Testament being healed of leprosy and having to go through these rituals. Not one! Not until Jesus enters the scene in the New Testament. Then watch out! The priesthood had never had to go through this ritual until then. I’m sure they had to refresh themselves on the law because they were VERY busy when Jesus started healing people! Jesus makes all the difference! He came to fulfill the law!
