The fire is NEVER allowed to go out. The priests conducted the ‘olah offering (burnt offering) without fail. The worshipers did not offer these male sheep (rams) but were from a particular flock owned by the priesthood on behalf of Israel. They were raised with one purpose: to die. The ‘olah was given each morning and then every evening. It was the first and last sacrifice of the day. Each morning, the priest on duty would remove the ashes from the previous day’s sacrifices. 

```
“Then he is to take off these ____ and put on ___, and carry the ashes ___ the camp to a place that is ceremonially clean.” (Leviticus 6:11)
```
They would pile up the ashes next to the Brazen Altar, and then that same priest would remove his priestly garments and change them into another set before moving the ashes outside the camp. Outside the camp, those ashes were carried beyond the hundreds of thousands of tents that housed the Israelites. 

All these clothes…and of fine linen! Where did they get them while wandering in the desert? They couldn’t grow crops as they traveled. They did a lot of trading and business with other tribes during that time. You couldn’t hide 2-3 million people. It’s not like they were incognito in the dessert. As the nation of Israel moved, they bartered for what they needed. After they escaped Pharaoh’s army, they established contacts with traders and merchants. It was common knowledge where they were. They would barter for spices, food seasonings, olive oil, frankincense, dyes, cookware, animals, linen…so many things! And remember, they had a lot of money to trade with! 

The fire needed to remain burning always. OK…but where did it come from originally? 

What does Leviticus 9:24 tell us? ____

Without God lighting the original fire, it would have not been holy. They might as well have a big BBQ pit. 

Under no circumstances could the priests let the fire on the Brazen altar go out. Yahweh started it, and they could not offer sacrifices if it went out. The blood and fire were intertwined, making atonement possible. Man-made fire was utterly unsuitable. 

Paul writes in 1 Thessalonians 5:19, “Do not ____ the Spirit.” He could very well have used the analogy of the holy fire on the Burnt offering altar. God sent His Son and the Holy Spirit to man, and that Holy Fire was placed in every believer. If God’s Holy Fire is quenched in us, nothing can be done to replace it with man’s power. 

Another great thing about that fire never being put out was that God was available for forgiveness at any time, day or night. There was always access, no matter who you were. The fire is always burning. When you are ready to approach God with your sacrifice, He awaits you. 

Do you keep the Holy Fire of Yahweh burning in your heart of worship? I mean, to be honest, some days are brighter than others. Right? But we need to rekindle that fire ourselves. How do we do that? How do you rekindle any fire? You put fuel on it. 

If you fall asleep when you pray, go to bed earlier so you’re not so tired. Or wake up a little earlier to do your devotions in the morning. You are fueling your fire by being in this study! You are fueling your fire by serving the Lord, reading the Scriptures, and participating in worship. What are some other ways you can think of? ___

Why do you think that God reiterates the various offerings in this chapter? He just told Moses about them. ____

We need to know that God sets the rules. It’s not by our understanding. You can’t approach God thoughtlessly or carelessly—especially if you are a priest. (Keep that in mind as we get to chapter 10 when Aaron’s sons bring strange fire before the Lord.)

Over and over in this chapter, God is giving instructions to Aaron and his sons. While all of Aaron's descendants are Levites, not all Levites are Aaron’s descendants. Aaron’s sons are called the Cohen… priests. If a person is a Cohen, they are a priest from the line of Aaron. Even though the Levites were a priestly tribe, only one family qualified as priests, and that was the descendants of Aaron. The other Levite families and their descendants also served in the Tabernacle and later the Temple, doing other duties. But they could not officiate in the sacrificial rituals.

Imagine stumbling upon an old, dusty portrait in your family’s attic. The colors are faded, and the details are not entirely clear, but as you gaze upon it and study the picture a little deeper, you recognize a resemblance to someone familiar. As you clean the dust away, you realize it’s a portrait of your great-great-grandparents, and suddenly, the blurred lines become a beautiful connection to your own story. 

In the same way, Leviticus might seem like an ancient painting with unclear details, but within its lines, there’s a portrait of the Messiah waiting to be revealed. The sacrificial system, the shedding of blood, and the fellowship offerings are all like brushstrokes, hinting at a much more profound and vivid image that would be revealed in Christ. 

God invites us to explore the shadows of the Messiah in our everyday moments. When we share a meal with our friends or console someone hurting, we live out the fellowship offering in our lives.

# Day 4 &middot; _Calling and Cleansing_

<span style="font-size: 1.25em"><i class="fa-solid fa-book-open"></i>&nbsp; **Read** |</span> Leviticus 8:1-6.

&nbsp;

Chapters 8, 9, and 10 are a unit. These three chapters will discuss the ordination of the first priesthood of Israel. So far in Leviticus, they’ve been given many rules and regulations for both the worshiper and the priests, but no actions have been taken yet. As of this chapter, no priesthood exists, but it is about to begin! 

It’s incredible to think we are looking at the first congregation—the church God called to Himself. But this church wasn’t a brick-and-mortar building. Instead, it was a tent that they carried with them wherever they went. 

```
“But you are a ___ people, a royal ____, a holy nation, God’s ___ possession, that you may declare the ___ of him who called you out of darkness into his wonderful ___.” (1 Peter 2:9)
```

Leviticus 8 is the beginning of our story as a royal priesthood! 

God “commands” Moses regarding these instructions 11 times in this chapter, ALONE! Moses wasn’t a bystander in Aaron and his sons' ordination service.

Since Exodus 20, God has provided a concrete and detailed structure for worship by Yahweh. God is making Israel a holy nation, set apart for Himself. He is introducing the concept of being holy. Here’s the deal: The people had to obey the commands and adhere to the structure, and in doing so, they would receive God’s blessing. But the blessing would be removed if they disobeyed or rebelled against God’s instructions. It sounds easy enough, right? 

But their obedience had to be exact. God was not interested in sloppy or passive worship. And we serve that same God. He is still not interested in sloppy or passive worship. Why? Why does it have to be like this, with all the attention to the smallest detail? The only answer is that it’s God’s rules, not man’s. 

There was no idea of, “You know, I don’t feel like bringing a sacrifice to the Lord today. It’s a great day for the beach!” or “I just don’t feel like going to the Tabernacle. I have a lot to do.” God wouldn’t accept that. You had to respond to Him in obedience. 

Obedience is the key to our relationship with God. Don’t be fooled into thinking that just because Jesus came, we do not have to obey God. How we conduct our lives is not a free-for-all because Jesus died for us. 

Jesus came to fulfill the Law, not to do away with it. He did not come to exchange law for grace. Yes, He did bring grace! But it is not an exchange for our obedience. Some say, “Well, Jesus didn’t actually say we have to obey.” Really? John says that Jesus is the Word… ALL the Word and He was in existence before the world began. What is the Word? It is the entire Word of God. When John penned this, the only Word of God written was the Torah and the Old Testament. The New Testament did not exist. 

The book of Hebrews is like a commentary on Leviticus. You have to understand Leviticus to comprehend Hebrews fully. 

```
“Through Jesus, therefore, let us ___ offer to God a ___ of praise—the fruit of lips that openly profess His name.”  (Hebrews 13:15)
```

You may say, “I don’t really like singing; it’s not my style. I want to sit there and look at the words.” Well, we are commanded to bring a sacrifice of praise. Not because you “feel” like it but because it’s a sacrifice of praise. Our approach to God in worship must include sacrifice. And that sacrifice of worship, according to Leviticus 8-10, must also include service. The focus in chapter 8 is not the sacrifice but the one who offers it. These priests got the honor and privilege to be mediators for the people before the Lord! Looking at it that way, it’s not that hard of a sacrifice.  

And here’s something to think about. It was not God’s original plan to have only the tribe of Levi be the priests. Initially, He wanted the entire nation to be a kingdom of priests. (See Exodus 19.) But God’s plan was changed when the people disobeyed and created a golden calf. How often do we miss out on God’s best for our lives when we decide to have it our way?

In verse 6, Moses washes Aaron and his sons with water. The water comes from the laver in the Tabernacle, which represents the Word of God. 

```
 “…to make her holy, cleansing her by the ____ with water through the ____.” (Ephesians 5:26)
```

Also see Titus 3:5, 1 John 1:9, and Hebrews 10:22. We are washed and cleansed by the Word of God.
