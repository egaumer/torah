Let's review the line of the priesthood. Jacob has 12 sons. One of his sons, Levi, had three sons: Gershon, Kohath, and Merari. God chose Aaron (Kohath's grandson) and Aaron's descendants as high priests from all the Levites. There were three Levites classes: the Kohan, the Levi, and the Israelite. While every Levite is an Israelite (a descendant of Israel), not every Levite (descendent of the Levi) is a Kohen—(a descendant of Aaron). The summary and chart are from www.hebrew4christians.com.

```
Examine Leviticus 21:5-6. “Priests must ___ shave their heads or shave off the edges of their beards or cut their bodies. They must be ___ to their God and must not ___ the name of their God. Because they present the food offerings to the Lord, the food of their God, they are to be ___.”
```

Did you know that the priesthood was not God’s original intention? God’s original intention was for the entire nation to have an intimate relationship with him, and the whole nation would be priests. God Himself is speaking in Exodus 19:5-6: “Now if you obey me fully and keep my covenant, then out of all nations you will be my ____ possession. Although the whole earth is mine, you will be for me a ___ of priests and a holy nation.”

Well then, what happened? Idolatry happened. The golden calf happened. It was so offensive to God that He selected only one tribe, Levi, and only one family from Levi, Aaron, to be the high priesthood. 

In chapter 21, God will set higher standards for the priests. Why? The priesthood is the link, the go-between, and the mediator between the people and God. 

The priests must be holy as God is holy to live near God's presence in the Tabernacle. The priesthood is one of the main ways holiness was maintained in Israel. 

Not only were the priests to be holy, but the sacrifices they brought to the altar needed to be without spot or blemish. These rules teach us so much about the beauty and sacredness of Jesus' sacrifice.


Jesus is not only the perfect priest who brings the sacrifice without blemish; He is the spotless, perfect sacrifice Himself!


As the perfect priest and sacrifice, Jesus took our sickness, deformities, sin, and disease onto Himself.  Read Isaiah 53:4-6 aloud and let this beautiful prophecy give you joy.

God instructed Moses to disallow anyone with a defect from completing priestly duties, but in His mercy, He still allowed them to participate.

```
Leviticus 21:22-23  “He may eat the most holy food of his God, as well as the holy food; yet because of his ___, he must not go ___ the curtain or ___ the altar, and so desecrate my sanctuary. I am the Lord, who makes them holy.”
```

There were stricter rules for the priests, just like requirements that were more stringent for those in ministry in the New Testament. 1 Timothy 3:1-13 gives us a long list of what is required. The ministry is a high calling. It’s good work, but it’s dangerous. For those who think, “Oh, I want to be a minister because they only work one day a week,”…you have no idea what being in the ministry is like! Ha! Beyond the busyness of the congregation, disgruntled people, managing personalities, and your family living in a fishbowl, you also become a dedicated target of the enemy. 

Charles Spurgeon had an excellent answer for people who said, “I want to be a pastor.” He said, “If you can be happy doing anything else, do it. It’s only if you can’t be happy, can’t be satisfied, you are driven and compelled by God to do it, then do it. But if you can be happy doing anything else, please do it.”

# Day 3 &middot; _The Sabbath and the Spring Holy Days_

This chapter is all about the appointed festivals. God loves a good party! 

```
Leviticus 23:2 “Speak to the Israelites and say to them: ‘These are my ___ festivals, the appointed festivals of the LORD, which you are to proclaim as sacred ___.”
```

In Hebrew, the word for assemblies is convocation. It’s a word we most see in terms of graduations. It is simply an assembly in English. But this verse says a “holy” or “sacred” assembly, so it refers specifically to a church service. There’s one more thing about this word that is so awesome, and I hope you don’t ever forget this…In Hebrew (the original language), it means “rehearsal.” All the times the Israelites worshipped at the Tabernacle, they rehearsed for a more significant future event.

When Carol Anne was younger, she was in many theater productions. She loved rehearsal time—getting things just right: learning lines, dances, blocking…it was during these rehearsal times that she made friends with her castmates, learned to work together towards a common goal, and followed the director’s instructions. The director’s job was to make the cast the best they could be for the show. 

**The Sabbath ** 

<span style="font-size: 1.25em"><i class="fa-solid fa-book-open"></i>&nbsp; **Read** |</span> Leviticus 23:1-3.

&nbsp;

In the rhythm of creation, God wove a sacred thread—a day of rest, a Sabbath. It is a holy pause amid the hustle and bustle of our lives. 

Full disclosure - I am a lover of the Sabbath. I live as Martha all week and let all things go to be Mary each 7th day of the week. These incredible rhythms that have blessed my life came through much learning and transformation. Initially, my family and I "kept the Sabbath” and appointed feasts for 20 years—legalistically. For the last 25 years, I have been blessed to delight in the Sabbath in the grace of the Lord. The story of our denominational transformation is incredible, but that is for another time and place. I am telling you this ahead of time because my presentation of the Sabbath and Holy Days will be with a personal bias and loving experience. It's my pleasure to share.

In verse 3, we revisit the Sabbath. We have already studied it in Exodus and Leviticus 16 and 19. It will be emphasized ten more times in Leviticus alone. It must be imperative to God to repeat it so much. 

We know that Jesus kept the Sabbath with His disciples and constantly showed how legalistic they were while never recognizing the Lord of the Sabbath. Do a word search of the Sabbath in an online Bible and see how many times it's mentioned in the Gospels alone. __________

Sabbath observation is a two-edged sword. It can be a tradition of men or a gift from God. When practiced in delight, the benefits are health, rest, worship, family time, a study in the Word, and awestruck wonders of what God has done each week. How awesome is it that our God gives us one vacation day a week?! 

**The Spring Holy Days**

<span style="font-size: 1.25em"><i class="fa-solid fa-book-open"></i>&nbsp; **Read** |</span> Leviticus 23:4-22.

&nbsp;

**Passover**

The Israelites came to Jerusalem three times a year for the annual festivals of the Lord. (Deuteronomy 16:16) Two journeys were in the spring and one in the fall. The first pilgrimage was for the Passover, the Feast of Unleavened Bread, and the Feast of the First Fruits. Psalms 120-134 are known as the Songs of Ascent, as the Israelites sang as they traveled to Jerusalem for God's appointed times.

The Holy Days of the Lord point to the plan of salvation for all the nations. Passover is the beginning of the feasts, and its Hebrew name is “Pesach,” meaning “to leave or spare by passing over.” The Passover was observed in remembrance of their deliverance out of Egypt and the sparing of their firstborn by the shedding of blood. We celebrate the Passover in remembrance of what God has done for His people through the blood of our Passover Lamb, Jesus.

```
1 Corinthians 5:7 - “Get rid of the old yeast, so that you may be a new unleavened batch—as you really are. For Christ, our Passover __, has been sacrificed.”
```

Many Christians desire to gather for a Passover. Those who want to experience a traditional Seder should consider visiting a local synagogue, a Messianic Jewish congregation, or a Jewish ministry to ask permission to participate in the Passover celebration. Other Christians celebrate Passover with the New Testament symbols of bread and wine, our Lord's body and blood, and the washing of other's feet as Jesus did for His disciples. (Option: There is a link to Carol Anne’s message on the Passover at the end of this section.)

Jesus fulfilled this Scripture when He became the ultimate Passover Lamb whose sacrificial death brings liberation from the bondage of sin and death.

**The Days of Unleavened Bread**

Immediately following Passover (after sundown), the Days of Unleavened Bread is celebrated for seven days. The first day of Unleavened Bread (sundown to sundown) is a High Sabbath that falls on different days each calendar year. It is not the same as the weekly Sabbath. There can be two Sabbaths during the feast in the same week. Only unleavened bread is eaten during these seven days, commemorating Israel's hurried departure from Egypt. Paul reminded the early church to keep the Feast. Removing leaven represents the removal of sin. Leaven puffs up. It ferments and decays as sin brings death.

```
1 Corinthians 5:8 - “Therefore let us keep the Festival, not with the old bread ___ with malice and wickedness, but with the ___ bread of sincerity and truth.”
```

When our family celebrated the Passover and the Days of Unleavened Bread, we would take a whole week to prepare our home. Removing all the leaven is an impossible prospect, teaching us how impossible it is to rid ourselves of our sins. It was so difficult that I threw out our toaster each year! When we gathered for the Passover celebration, we celebrated with communion, the body and blood of our Savior, and then washed each other's feet. We each had a basin with warm water. The woman went into one room—the men in another. We randomly paired off and began the unforgettable experience of humbly serving each other as our Lord served His disciples. I highly recommend this to you.

The unleavened bread is associated with Jesus' sinless nature. His body, broken for us, is symbolized by unleavened bread in Christian Communion.

**The Feast of Firstfruits**

Read Leviticus 23:9-14. While the Israelites were still in Jerusalem, they celebrated the First Fruits offering, the day after the Sabbath within the Feast. This part of the feast was an act that symbolized that the first and best of everything belongs to God and that Israel would put the Lord first in every part of life. It is also an expression of thanksgiving for God's gift of the early harvest supplying their daily bread. 

Jesus is called the “first fruits" in the New Testament (1 Corinthians 15:20-23). His resurrection is the fulfillment. God gave His best for us; He gave it all. In this verse, Paul links Firstfruits to Jesus' resurrection. Jesus is the Bread of Life!

According to Tom Bradford (Torah Class), the most central stories about Jesus took place under the backdrop of one or another of the Biblical festivals, such as pilgrimage and Biblical festivals. So the Bible had Jesus going to Jerusalem to participate in these various Scriptural holy days, as every good and dedicated Jew would do. (See the end of this week’s resources for more information.)

**Pentecost - The Feast of Weeks (Pentecost)**

<span style="font-size: 1.25em"><i class="fa-solid fa-book-open"></i>&nbsp; **Read** |</span> Leviticus 23:15-22.

&nbsp;


Even though the name Pentecost or Feast of Weeks is not mentioned in this passage, you can find its title in Exodus 34:22, Numbers 28:26, and Deuteronomy 16:9-10. “The Feast of Weeks” is also known as the “Feast of the Ingathering.” We know it today as Pentecost, the fiftieth day in Greek.

The counting of days to arrive at this next pilgrimage to the Temple begins with the Feast of First Fruits. 
```
“‘Count off ___ days up to the day after the seventh Sabbath, and then present an offering of new grain to the Lord.” (Leviticus 23:16)
```

From a physical aspect, Pentecost celebrated the summer harvest, the second harvest of the year. From a spiritual aspect—it was the day that Jesus sent the Holy Spirit to dwell within men. The first harvest, Firstfruits, gathered Jesus into the Kingdom of God. The second harvest, Pentecost, gathered men into the Kingdom of God. Jewish tradition celebrates the giving of the Ten Commandments (the Law) on Pentecost. As Christians, we celebrate the Law Giver, Who promises to place the law within us and write it upon our hearts. 

```
Jeremiah 31:33: “I will put my ___ in their ___ and write it on their ___. I will be their God, and they will be my people.”
```

Most importantly, we gather for Pentecost in awe, as Jesus told his followers to wait in Jerusalem for the promise of the Holy Spirit.

On a personal note, Pentecost was one of the most challenging gatherings for me as a legalist. It was typically the only Sunday that a Sabbath followed the weekly Sabbath, and because it fell at the end of May or early June, it was right at the end of the school year. As a teacher, the close out of the school year is crazy, hectic, and full of much work. Since both these days of the weekend were Sabbaths and no customary work was allowed, I was at wit's end. As our denomination was transforming from law to grace, I'll never forget when one of our most beloved gentlemen in the church told me, "Karen, Christ tore the veil in two for you also." Praise God for His mercy and grace. 

The Spring Holy Days teach us that Jesus Christ, our Passover Lamb, was sinless and the true Lamb of God. He gave His life so that the sins of humanity could be forgiven and the death penalty removed. (1 Corinthians 5:7; 1 Peter 1:18-20; Romans 3:25) The Feast of Unleavened Bread teaches us that we have been called to reject lawlessness and repent of sin. Leaven represents sin and is removed from our homes as Christ removes our sins as far as the East is from the West. We proclaim sinless Jesus as our Bread of Life by eating unleavened bread. The Feast of First Fruits and Pentecost celebrate Jesus as the first fruit of all who will be resurrected and the indwelling of the Holy Spirit. Jesus came to establish His church—which we celebrate at Pentecost. The first fruits are those who will be given salvation at the return of Christ. Come, Lord Jesus, come.

Note: All of the Spring festivals have been fulfilled with Jesus' life, death, and resurrection. We now await the fulfillment of the fall festivals.
